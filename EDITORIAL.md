# Hara Learn editorial policy

## Mission

Hara Learn makes serious work around Hara, Lisp, language implementation, runtime systems, and programmable media easier to discover and understand. It is allowed to advocate strongly. It is not allowed to disguise provenance, automation, sponsorship, or uncertainty.

“Propaganda” is used internally in its literal sense: material intended to propagate ideas. Public work should be persuasive through clarity, demonstration, and evidence rather than manipulation.

## Content labels

Every article uses one of four labels:

- **Dispatch** — original Hara Learn editorial or essay.
- **Release** — an official project announcement or release note.
- **Field note** — practical, provisional, or experimental technical writing.
- **Syndicated** — writing first published elsewhere and carried under a registered permission mode.

A community article is not an official Hara position unless it is explicitly marked and authorised as one.

## Syndication

An RSS or Atom feed is a transport, not a republication licence.

- `link`: metadata and a short Hara Learn introduction; readers continue to the original.
- `excerpt`: a limited quotation or feed-provided excerpt, attribution, and canonical link.
- `full`: full-text republication under owner permission, explicit authorisation, or a compatible open licence.

Excerpt is the default. Full-text permission must be recorded before activation. A source owner can request correction, pause, or removal through the registry contact path or a repository issue.

## Automation

Automation may:

- normalise feed metadata;
- propose summaries and titles for review;
- generate platform-specific copy;
- generate storyboards, narration scripts, captions, and visual templates;
- create draft emails, private video uploads, and review queues;
- record publication receipts.

Automation may not:

- fabricate quotations, contributors, demonstrations, adoption, or benchmarks;
- remove author attribution or the canonical URL;
- present synthetic narration as an undisclosed human performance;
- publish a newly discovered feed without registry and editorial approval;
- repeatedly post minor variations merely to manufacture engagement;
- overwrite corrections or silently rewrite a writer’s position.

## Corrections

Material factual errors should be corrected in the canonical article with an `updatedAt` date and a visible correction note when material. Derivative posts should be updated where practical or should point to the correction. Deleting the evidence of a material correction is not the default response.

## Video

Automated shorts should be restrained, technically legible, and visibly Hara. They should prefer code, diagrams, product footage, typography, and material visual motifs over unrelated stock media. Synthetic voice use must be disclosed in the description or edition metadata.

Private review is the default YouTube state. A human checks title, claims, captions, pronunciation, visuals, licensing, and links before public release.

## Mailing lists

The main list is a curated digest, not a mirror of every repository event. Initial target cadence:

- one weekly Hara Learn Dispatch;
- optional lower-volume release-only list later;
- no purchased addresses;
- clear unsubscribe and archive access through the selected provider;
- newsletter content generated as portable Markdown before provider upload.

## Social distribution

The objective is useful transmission, not universal simultaneous posting. Channel copy may differ to respect format and context, but attribution and canonical links remain. Networks requiring creator confirmation or special review should receive drafts or upload packages rather than blind automatic publication.

## Sponsorship and conflicts

Paid placement, affiliate relationships, donated services, and material author conflicts must be disclosed near the relevant content. Sponsorship does not buy an unlabelled technical conclusion.
